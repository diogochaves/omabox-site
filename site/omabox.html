2parallel boxes
Every agent gets a desktop. None of them is yours.

        
Without omabox, an agent testing a desktop app tests it on your desktop. With it, each agent takes a box of its own: four agents, four apps, four test runs, all at once, while you keep typing.

On their own
Agents take a box when a task needs a desktop, wait until a window has settled instead of sleeping, and put it down when they exit. No prompt stops them: in a box, pkexec just says no.

        
Checks side by side
Tests, visual checks and videos run in parallel, each on its own screen and session bus. None sees another's windows, and none sees yours.

        
Handed on
A lead agent gives each subagent a box and omabox brief: about 15 lines with its windows and the rules, not the whole skill.

      

    


    

      

        
3ask your agent

4uses
A desktop to spare, for whatever you're making.

        
Testing is where omabox started. Any job that needs a screen and shouldn't touch yours fits in a box too: these are a few we use it for.

      

      

        
● RECopen the panelscene 2

          
Film what you made
A demo of your app, plugin or feature, filmed in a box while you keep working. Your marks become captions; clicks and keys are drawn in.

          
$ omabox record start -o demo.mp4
$ omabox record mark "scene 2"
$ omabox record stop --show-input --captions

        
Aa1xAa3840x2160 · 2x

          
Shots at 2x, or 4K
A box is whatever size and scale you ask for, whatever your monitor is. Crisp shots and videos for a README, a store page or a talk.

          
$ omabox up --size 3840x2160,scale=2
$ omabox shot --window app

        
12:00 · alwaysa stock Omarchy
your bar and theme left out

          
A clean setup, not yours
A stock bar and theme, a clock that never moves, nothing on screen but what you show. Keep a terminal out of the shot: its prompt still has your user and host names.

          
$ omabox up --clean --stock-bar --theme tokyo-night

        
portraitscale 1.6

          
Monitors you don't have
Portrait, scaled, side by side or below. Plug and unplug them while the box runs, and see what your shell does.

          
$ omabox up --monitor 1080x1920 --monitor 2560x1440,scale=1.6,below

        

          
Look at a web page, off your screen
Your agent opens a site in the box's browser, pages down and reads the shots. No browser window lands on your desktop.

          
$ omabox run -d --wait -- chromium --app=URL
$ omabox keys Page_Down

        
a monitor gone and back, over and over
until something falls over

          
Find the crash first
Unplug a monitor again and again until your shell or plugin falls over, then read the backtrace. Your session never notices.

          
$ omabox output drop --cycles 50
$ omabox gdb --shell

1.0.0 · 2026-10-27 · everything from 0.6 to 1.0 you would notice · changelog →

            
    
              
  • A contract your scripts can keep
    Within 1.x, exit codes and --json fields stay or only grow. A change warns for one release first; OMABOX_STRICT=1 makes it an error, for CI.
    • 
              
  • One binary, in Rust
    Stopped mid-step, omabox undoes that step: an interrupted up takes its box down, a stopped recording keeps its video.
    • 
              
  • Ports no box reaches
    Your database stays out of every box, on loopback or any of your host's addresses.
    $ omabox config protect-ports 5432,8080
    • 
              
  • The app answers in text
    tree, check, record and audio: an agent asks the app, compares it with a baseline, films it and hears it.
    • 
            

          

          

            
0.9.0 · 2026-10-20 · the last release before 1.0 · changelog →

            
    
              
  • A brief for a subagent
    About 15 lines a lead agent pastes into a subagent's prompt in place of the whole skill.
    $ omabox brief -b app-menu
    • 
              
  • Help that fits
    omabox help CMD is the command's lines and options; --long has the prose.
    • 
              
  • Exit codes that mean one thing
    Called wrong is exit 2, with the nearest option or command named, in every command.
    $ omabox help exit-codes
    • 
              
  • Holds in a video
    record hold pauses a take on the video's own clock; record --for films something that moves by itself.
    • 
            

          

          

            
0.8.0 · 2026-10-20 · visual checks · changelog →

            
    
              
  • Visual checks
    check compares an app's or a plugin's look with a baseline, in a box where only what is tested moves.
    $ omabox check run -- ./visual.sh
    • 
              
  • Your own Quickshell
    Run the box's shell on a Quickshell build of yours.
    $ omabox up --quickshell ~/src/quickshell/build
    • 
              
  • Your fonts, in every box
    Read-only, so an app draws in a box the way it draws for you.
    • 
              
  • Says what's in the way
    click --window says when a toast or another layer covers the point it aims at.
    • 
            

          

          

            
0.7.0 · 2026-10-16 · held keys, your binds · changelog →

            
    
              
  • Hold a key across commands
    Hold Shift, click three things, let go.
    $ omabox keys down:shift
    • 
              
  • Your binds, in the box
    A box loads your Hyprland bindings and input settings.
    • 
              
  • A journal in every box, CPU per thread
    Logs of what ran, and where the time went.
    • 
              
  • A crash says it crashed
    With the signal, so an agent doesn't wait on a window that will never come.
    • 
            

          

          

            
0.6.0 · 2026-10-14 · the app as text · changelog →

            
    
              
  • A window as text
    An app's controls as compact text, for a fraction of a shot's tokens.
    $ omabox tree --find 'dark mode'
    • 
              
  • Videos with sound and marks
    up --audio, record mark, and every monitor in one video.
    • 
              
  • Your whole shell in a box
    Every plugin and your shell.json, without touching your bar.
    $ omabox up --desk-shell

  • Boxes nobody will take down
    ls names them; down --orphans clears them.

0.5.0 · 2026-10-09 · several monitors in one box · changelog →

            
    
              
  • Several monitors in one box
    As many as a test needs, any size, scale and place, a bar on each.
    $ omabox up --monitor 1080x1920
    • 
              
  • A monitor that drops off and comes back
    output drop --cycles, until your shell crashes; gdb gives the backtrace.

6built for agents
Everything an agent needs to test its work, and nothing of yours.

      

        
One box per session
Named after its worktree and session. Up in 3 to 5 s, about 400 to 600 MB, down when the agent exits. Subagents get a box and a brief each.
$ omabox brief -b app-5cc72cdc
# the lines a subagent needs, not the whole skill
How it works →

        
Answers in text
After a click, omabox says what changed, or that nothing did. tree reads the app's controls, where a shot costs far more.

a shot~1.5k

Checks and videos
check keeps what three runs agree on as the baseline, then names the button that moved. record films a take with captions and a contact sheet.

7ai-jail
Not a sandbox. Pair it with ai-jail.

        
omabox keeps an agent's windows off your desktop, but it is not a security boundary: a box shares your kernel, GPU and network. ai-jail, by Fabio Akita, fences what the agent itself can read, write and reach. Together, a jailed agent tests in boxes of its own.

      

      

        

          
your machine
            
ai-jail
              
the agentclaude, codex, opencode…

              
rw ~/code/app
its API, nothing else

              
~/.ssh ~/.config your LAN

            

            
omabox
brokerup · run · shot

            
its boxisolated
./build/app

          

          
ai-jail decides what the agent can touch. omabox decides where it draws.

        

        

          
A jailed app, drawn in a box


$ omabox up
$ eval "$(omabox env)"
$ ai-jail --gpu --rw-map "$WAYLAND_DISPLAY" \
    --env WAYLAND_DISPLAY -- ./build/app

          
A jailed agent, driving boxes of its own


$ omabox broker on   # the lines for ~/.ai-jail
$ ai-jail claude

          
Tested with ai-jail 2.10.0 and 2.12.0 and omabox 1.0. A jailed agent can't use host, peek or interactive boxes.

9mit
MIT, every line. It's your computer.

        
omabox is built by builders, to make other builders' lives easier. All of it is MIT: read it, fork it, bend it to your machine, ship your own. Every fix someone sends makes it better for the next person. That's why it won't stay on Omarchy alone: the builders on Hyprland are who it's for.
